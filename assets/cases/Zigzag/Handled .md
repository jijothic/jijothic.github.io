# Handled — Making employee onboarding and offboarding simpler

**End-to-end brand and product design for a more human approach to IT operations.**

**Client:** Handled  
**Year:** 2026  
**Duration:** 4 weeks  
**Role:** Brand & Product Designer  
**Scope:** Brand strategy, naming, visual identity, UX/UI design, website design, illustration, and templates  
**Status:** Designed and launched

## Overview

Employee onboarding and offboarding involve more than adding or removing an employee from a system. These processes span departments, tools, permissions, and administrative tasks. When they're fragmented, work becomes repetitive, coordination gets harder, and access can remain in place long after someone leaves.

Handled set out to simplify this experience by bringing employee lifecycle management into one cohesive platform.

The challenge was twofold: create a distinctive brand in a crowded, technically focused market and translate that identity into a digital product that feels approachable, clear, and easy to understand.

I led most of the design work across the brand, website, and product interface, building a connected experience from the first brand interaction through to the platform itself.

## The challenge

Integration and IT management software often communicates through technical terminology, complex diagrams, and infrastructure-focused messaging. While this language can make sense to technical teams, it can make the underlying value harder for the wider organization to understand.

Handled addressed a broader operational need. Employee onboarding and offboarding affect IT, HR, Finance, and Operations, yet the experience is often distributed across tools and manual processes.

The opportunity was to make this complexity feel manageable.

Rather than presenting another technical platform, the design needed to communicate clarity, ownership, and simplicity — both in how the product looked and how the brand explained its value.

## 01 — Defining the brand

### A name that communicates the outcome

The project began with naming and brand strategy.

Several potential names were explored, including Fluxfuse, Hendlee, Cierge, and Skwit. The chosen name, **Handled**, communicated the product's promise directly: the process is taken care of, without requiring users to manage every detail themselves.

The name established a clear foundation for the brand — practical, confident, and focused on outcomes rather than technical complexity.

### Building a more human identity

With the name established, I developed a visual direction that deliberately moved away from the conventional look of enterprise integration software.

Instead of relying on restrained corporate palettes, generic typography, or abstract technology imagery, the identity uses bright colors, expressive typography, and a recognizable character named Jimmy.

Jimmy gives the brand a human presence. Rather than communicating through technical diagrams alone, the identity makes the idea of having a process under control more tangible and approachable.

The result is a visual system designed to stand apart from the category while reinforcing the product's core promise.

## 02 — Translating the brand into a digital experience

### Designing the website

The website introduces the problem before presenting the solution.

Its narrative starts with the everyday frustrations of onboarding and offboarding, helping visitors understand the problem Handled addresses before explaining the platform.

This approach keeps the communication grounded in recognizable operational challenges rather than leading with technical features and integrations.

The visual identity carries through the experience, creating continuity between the brand's personality and the product's positioning.

### Designing the product interface

The platform needed to feel like a natural extension of the brand, not a separate enterprise application.

I carried the visual language into the product interface, using the established identity to create a consistent experience across the brand and platform. Jimmy also appears within the product, extending the brand's personality beyond the marketing website.

The product's intended experience centers on bringing employee lifecycle management into one place, making the process easier to follow and reducing the burden of coordinating disconnected tasks.

The design direction balances approachability with the clarity and control expected from a business application.

**Design principle:** make the experience feel manageable without making the underlying operational complexity invisible.

## 03 — Creating a cohesive design system

A central part of the project was ensuring that the brand, website, and product felt like parts of the same experience.

The visual identity was not treated as an isolated logo or marketing asset. Its colors, typography, illustration style, and personality informed the wider digital experience.

This consistency helps connect three different contexts:

- **Brand:** establishes a recognizable personality and communicates the product's promise.
- **Website:** introduces the problem and explains the value of the solution.
- **Platform:** carries that promise into the product experience.

By designing these touchpoints together, I could maintain a coherent visual direction rather than allowing the marketing experience and product interface to drift apart.

## The outcome

Handled launched with a distinctive brand identity, a website, and a product interface built around a shared design direction.

The project delivered:

- A brand name and positioning centered on simplicity and ownership.
- A visual identity designed to distinguish Handled from conventional enterprise software.
- A website that leads with recognizable customer problems before introducing the solution.
- A product interface that extends the brand's visual language into the platform.
- A consistent experience across brand, marketing, and product touchpoints.

The key outcome was a unified digital presence that communicates the same idea at every stage: employee onboarding and offboarding should feel handled, not complicated.

## Reflection

Handled was an exercise in connecting brand thinking with digital product design.

The challenge was not simply to make enterprise software look different. It was to establish a clear product promise and carry it consistently through the identity, website, and interface.

Designing these elements together helped create a more cohesive experience, where the brand's personality and the product's purpose reinforce one another.

The project reinforced the value of treating brand and product design as connected parts of the same system — especially in categories where technical complexity can overshadow the value users are actually looking for.
