Curadora

Hotel search for fans

Client: Curadora

Timespan: '23

Key Focus: App Design

Fans don't book hotels the way everyone else does. They book around a date, a venue, and a crowd that's about to descend on the same three blocks. Generic travel search treats a concert weekend the same as a random Tuesday - sorted by price, blind to the one thing that actually matters: how close you'll be when the show lets out at midnight. Curadora set out to fix that, building a hotel search built entirely around the event, not the destination.

What we did

We joined Curadora as the in-house product design team, working in two-week sprints alongside engineering. Every sprint started from the same question: would this make a fan's trip easier the night before a show? Decisions were grounded in analytics from the existing search flow and interviews with fans who'd just come back from a trip - what they booked, what they regretted, and what they wished the app had told them sooner.

Search, anchored to the event

The old flow started with a city and a date range - a destination-first model borrowed from every other travel app. We flipped it: search starts with the artist, team, or venue, and Curadora pulls the exact show date and address automatically. Results are sorted by walk time to the door, not just distance on a map, and flag which blocks get loud, which get quiet, and which sell out fastest once doors open.

Map view with live walk-time estimates, and a hotel detail screen that shows event-day pricing next to the normal rate - so surge pricing stops being a surprise at checkout.

Group-friendly booking for fans travelling together, late checkout flagged by default on show nights, and a one-tap way to rebook if an artist's set runs long and the last train's gone.

Result

Within the first two quarters after launch, average walk time between booked hotels and venues dropped by 22%, and event-day search sessions converted noticeably more often than the old destination-first flow. Curadora was featured as App of the Week in the Travel category, and - more importantly for the team - support tickets about "surprise" surge pricing nearly disappeared.
